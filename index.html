I build the platforms other builders stand on, and my motivation is watching them succeed — teams shipping value they could not have shipped without the thing underneath them. For six years that has meant developer platforms at scale: the framework, build infrastructure, observability and runtime core behind Switzerland's largest online retailer, carrying 50+ teams through millions to billions of requests a month and a rollout from two markets to eight — while making it one of the three fastest large-scale shops in Europe, from 2,500ms down to 1,000ms.

For the past eighteen months that platform has gone into AI end to end: the AI skill registry, the agentic frontend development stack, an agentic design IDE, and the evaluation harness that decides what is real. My thesis is that software delivery is becoming a framework concern — the leverage is no longer in writing code, but in defining the skills, the data loops and the interfaces through which autonomous systems do the work, and proving they did it correctly.

Measurement runs through all of it. I own experimentation, observability and user-behaviour monitoring; I build the KPI trees; I write the analytics code myself. For a period I drove how the organisation measures success — not what counts as winning, but how we would know.

The domains are ones this job cares about, and I have been on their floors. Across Galaxus, Celonis and HYVE I have worked in warehouses, production halls and manufacturing plants — building computer vision systems for Lidl warehouses and logistics systems at Celonis — on client projects with Dell, Lidl, Adidas, BMW, Mercedes, ZF, Wagner and Witron.

That is the software half. The other half started in robotics and computer vision at ETH and never fully stopped — I still build hardware on the side. This role is the one where both halves count for the same job.
